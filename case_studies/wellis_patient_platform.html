Interview case study / Product strategy

Wellis Patient Platform

How would I improve a patient platform without losing sight of the people already moving through it?

The problem I chose to address

The assignment asked for a target platform, a safe path towards it, and a way to judge progress. I focused on journey integrity: knowing where a case is, who is responsible for it, and whether the next hand-off has succeeded.

This was a response to an interview brief. It demonstrates my product reasoning, not employment at Wellis or delivery of a live patient platform.

My recommendation

I proposed starting with a small, controlled cohort and making the journey visible before expanding automation. My first increment, Case Control v0, would surface missing hand-offs, unowned exceptions and cases that need attention.

Normal cases should move without extra manual work. People should handle the uncertain or failed cases. I would put this capability inside the target platform when feasible. A temporary cross-system layer would only be justified if it could retire as the target platform took over.

The decisions that matter

  • Link records without merging responsibilities. Commercial records and clinical records serve different purposes. An explicit link should connect them.
  • Keep human decisions accountable. A doctor review and a pharmacist check remain separate gates. Automation should not imply either decision.
  • Make failure visible. A hand-off needs an acknowledgement. If it fails, someone must own the recovery.
  • Preserve the decision history. Original submissions, later amendments and the rules used should remain reconstructable.

A proposed delivery sequence

  1. Observe the workflow and establish a baseline before choosing the largest opportunity.
  2. Define identity, state, ownership and hand-off contracts with the relevant teams.
  3. Test a small cohort with explicit stop conditions and recovery paths.
  4. Expand only when the evidence supports it, then retire temporary components.

The trade-off

A cross-system view could improve visibility quickly. It could also become another source of truth or a permanent manual reconciliation task. I would reject that outcome. The durable product assets should be clear contracts and accountable decisions, not another dashboard people must keep updated.

How I would test the recommendation

I would measure unowned exceptions, ageing cases, failed acknowledgements and reconciliation effort. These are proposed measures, not achieved results. Clinical rules and release decisions would need approval from the accountable specialists.

What I took from the case

A product roadmap is incomplete if it only describes the destination. It also needs to explain how people keep receiving a reliable service while the systems change.

My contribution

Problem framing, product boundaries, a proposed first increment, delivery sequence and success measures.

Evidence boundary

Interview recommendation. Not deployed by me. No production outcome or patient metric is claimed.

Product strengths

  • Prioritisation under uncertainty
  • Human-in-the-loop design
  • Exception handling
  • Migration planning
