Aquablu Inventory and Replenishment

Helping a site manager decide what to reorder without confusing an order, a delivery and stock they can actually use.

Try the working demo

The brief and the problem I chose

Aquablu's Digital Product Builder business case asked for a working prototype from a supplied data pack and a walkthrough of the decisions and failure modes. I chose flavour inventory and replenishment for a connected dispenser site.

The practical question was what the manager should do next. Consumption, catalogue information and order status can help, but none of them alone proves how many spare boxes are in the cupboard.

Start with a stock state people can trust

I required an opening count rather than treating a historical count as current inventory. Receipt adds boxes to cupboard stock. A confirmed fresh installation removes a box. Dispatch and delivery status alone do not change the balance.

  1. Confirm stock
  2. Record receipt
  3. Confirm replacement
  4. Review replenishment
  5. Approve and track

A recommendation the manager can inspect

The prototype estimates daily use from recorded consumption and observed days, then combines that estimate with nominal box yield and catalogue lead time. A reorder threshold includes a safety buffer; a separate target determines the suggested quantity.

The manager can edit, approve or reject the suggestion. The calculation is deterministic and visible. I used AI to help build the prototype; an LLM does not make the inventory recommendation.

Build the exceptions into the journey

  • A delivery is short. Confirm quantities separately for each flavour. Keep the order incomplete until every line is received; record the discrepancy instead of adding missing boxes.
  • A flavour is unavailable. Keep the need visible and exclude that flavour from the new order. The demo does not invent a substitute or claim the shortage is resolved.
  • There is no consumption history. Track receipts for that flavour, but show that demand is unknown rather than generating a recommendation.
  • The manager rejects the suggestion. Retain the stock risk. A rejected recommendation does not mean that inventory improved.
  • Order creation fails or is retried. The mock integration reports failure without recording success and guards against duplicate creation within the demo cycle.

What I built

I used Codex to turn the product logic into a browser prototype with a guided next action, cupboard balances, order states and a short activity history. It supports opening counts, per-flavour receipts, replacement events and manager approval through a simulated webshop request.

The output was runnable software for the business-case discussion. It was not a slide presentation, a live Aquablu integration or a production deployment.

Where the prototype stops

Opening stock and new events are simulated. Safety-stock buffers are product assumptions, not validated Aquablu policies. Catalogue availability does not reserve warehouse stock, and nominal yields and average consumption do not guarantee future demand.

The guided replenishment journey covers one flavour and one order cycle. Multi-flavour receipt tracking is broader, but continuous planning, concurrent approvals, backorder reconciliation and combining orders across flavours remain production questions. Browser storage is local to the current device.

What I would validate next

I would observe who counts stock and confirms receipts at a site, then test whether the proposed tasks fit that person's day. With Aquablu, I would validate purchasing authority, demand quality, lead-time variation and the cost of combining or splitting orders before automating more decisions.

Proposed success measures include stock-count discrepancies, stockout incidents, time spent replenishing and recommendation acceptance or override reasons. No reduction in cost, stockouts or manual work was measured in this business case.

What this case changed

A useful replenishment product needs a defensible stock balance before it needs a more sophisticated forecast. The prototype made that trade-off concrete enough to discuss and challenge.

My contribution

Problem selection, workflow and state design, recommendation rules, exception handling and an AI-assisted working prototype.

Evidence boundary

Business-case work, not employment at Aquablu. Operational events and webshop responses are simulated. No production outcomes are claimed.
